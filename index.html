0.1 The governing rule

FSRH: contraception may be prescribed remotely only at UKMEC 1 or 2. This protocol is for contraception at UKMEC 1 and 2. It excludes UKMEC 3 and 4. EveryDayMeds follows that. CHC §13.2 · POP §19

0.2 Scope exclusions

Under-18s — see §3 for the reason.

Emergency contraception, LARC (implant, IUD/IUS), injectable, patch and vaginal ring — separate pathways, not covered here.

Contraception prescribed for a non-contraceptive indication alone — acne, heavy menstrual bleeding, endometriosis, PMDD, menopausal symptoms.

Continuation of a brand not on the formulary in Tables 1 and 2 without prescriber review.

Anyone requiring specialist contraceptive input.

1. Purpose and Scope

This protocol establishes the clinical and operational framework for the EveryDayMeds online contraception service, providing combined oral contraceptives (COCs) and progestogen-only pills (POPs) to adults aged 18 years and over.

It defines the requirements for patient assessment, eligibility, contraindications, treatment selection, prescribing, counselling and follow-up to ensure safe and consistent clinical practice.

All prescriptions must be reviewed and authorised by a registered independent prescriber following an individual clinical assessment.

1.1 Remote Prescribing and Clinical Assessment

Patients are assessed through a structured online consultation designed to identify relevant medical history, contraindications, risk factors and contraceptive needs.

All consultations are reviewed by an independent prescriber, who may contact the patient to obtain, clarify or verify information where necessary.

Patients who do not meet the eligibility criteria, require further clinical investigations or cannot be safely managed remotely will not be prescribed treatment and will be referred to their GP or an appropriate healthcare service.

The online assessment questionnaire is subject to validation and periodic review (Validation Register V6).

1.2 Pre-Prescribing Assessment Requirements

Combined oral contraceptives (COCs). Eligibility is determined through assessment of medical history, relevant risk factors, blood pressure and BMI, in accordance with the criteria established in §9.

Progestogen-only pills (POPs). Eligibility is determined through assessment of medical history, contraindications and relevant risk factors. Additional requirements for drospirenone-containing POPs are detailed in §11.

Patients requiring examinations or investigations outside the scope of the online service will be excluded from remote prescribing and referred for appropriate clinical assessment.

2. How the Service Routes

The service uses a single service-first consultation route: the patient selects contraception without preselecting a product. Named-product preselection is not offered.

Stage A — everyone

Service exclusions, red flags, and the clinical conditions that rule out both classes. A failure here means no contraception at all through this service. Nothing is offered as an alternative, because the reason for refusal is not method-specific. The §5.2 rules are the exception: they remove a single product or send both classes to review.

Stage B1 — COC

UKMEC 3 and 4 for combined hormonal contraception, the accumulation rule, oestrogen-relevant interactions, and the BP/BMI evidence standard. Failure rejects COC, or a single COC product (e.g. drospirenone COC).

Stage B2 — POP

UKMEC 3 and 4 for the progestogen-only pill, plus the drospirenone-specific renal and potassium rules. Failure rejects POP only, or the drospirenone product only.

These end the episode. No class is offered and no alternative product is suggested, because the reason for refusal is not method-specific. The one exception is the age row, which rejects a single class.

7. Stage B1 — The UKMEC 2 Accumulation Rule

7.1 Accumulation of UKMEC Risk Factors

UKMEC 2025 requires clinical judgement where multiple UKMEC 2 conditions contribute to the same clinical risk. Two or more cardiovascular risk factors may also meet the UKMEC 3 classification for multiple cardiovascular risk factors.

EveryDayMeds prescribing rule. Where two or more qualifying cardiovascular risk factors are identified, the consultation is held for mandatory prescriber review. The default outcome is do not prescribe COC and assess POP suitability, unless the prescriber documents why the combined risk is acceptable. UKMEC 2025 defines multiple risk factors as more than one, and requires clinical judgement where they coexist.

The same approach applies where multiple UKMEC 2 conditions contribute to another shared clinical risk. Such cases require prescriber review rather than automatic approval.

Patients with UKMEC 3 or 4 conditions are excluded from the affected contraceptive class under this protocol and should be offered an appropriate alternative where clinically suitable.

7.3 Worked example — Why cumulative-risk assessment is required

Clinical scenario. A 34-year-old woman requests a combined oral contraceptive. She smokes 10 cigarettes daily, has a BMI of 33 kg/m² and has normal blood pressure. She has no history of VTE or other identified contraindications.

Individually, smoking below age 35 and BMI 30–34.9 kg/m² are both CHC 2. However, both contribute to cardiovascular risk and must be considered together rather than assessed in isolation.

Prescriber action. The consultation flags the patient for cumulative-risk assessment under §7.2. The prescriber evaluates the combined risk and determines whether the UKMEC category for multiple cardiovascular risk factors (CHC 3) applies. If confirmed, COC must not be prescribed through EveryDayMeds and the patient should be assessed for POP suitability. The presence of two factors must not automatically assign UKMEC 3 without clinical judgement.

Status of the risk-screening rules. The cardiovascular and VTE factor lists in §7.2 are EveryDayMeds screening tools, not validated UKMEC scoring systems. Their purpose is to identify patients requiring additional clinical assessment. The final UKMEC classification must be based on the patient’s overall risk, with the rationale documented.

Alternative contraception. Where COC is excluded because of cumulative cardiovascular risk, POP may remain suitable. UKMEC 2025 classifies POP use with multiple cardiovascular risk factors as category 2 and with multiple VTE risk factors as category 1. The prescriber must still complete the full POP eligibility assessment, including any product-specific precautions.

Important. Any individual UKMEC 3 or 4 exclusion identified under §6 takes precedence over cumulative-risk screening. A first-degree family history of VTE, for example, is already CHC 3 and must not be treated as a risk factor requiring a second finding before COC is excluded.

14.1 Before starting — pregnancy and EC assessment

Record the first day and usual pattern of the last normal menstrual period, every episode of recent unprotected sexual intercourse (UPSI), current/recent contraception and whether it was used correctly. First assess whether EC is indicated.

Provided there are no symptoms or signs of pregnancy, pregnancy can be reasonably excluded when at least one applies:

  • No intercourse since the start of the last normal menstrual period or since childbirth, abortion, miscarriage or ectopic pregnancy.
  • A reliable method has been used correctly and consistently.
  • The patient is within the first 5 days of a normal natural menstrual period.
  • The patient is less than 21 days postpartum.
  • The patient is fully breastfeeding, amenorrhoeic and less than 6 months postpartum.
  • The patient is within 5 days after abortion, miscarriage or ectopic pregnancy.
  • There has been no intercourse for more than 21 days and a high-sensitivity urine pregnancy test (HSUPT) is negative.

18.1 Bleeding expectations and management
Traditional LNG/NET POP

Bleeding remains unpredictable, but normal-frequency bleeding is common and amenorrhoea is uncommon. In LNG study data at about 12 months, amenorrhoea occurred in about 2%.

Desogestrel 75 µg

At about 12 months, roughly 2–3 in 10 users may be amenorrhoeic, about 3 in 10 have infrequent bleeding and about 4 in 10 normal-frequency bleeding. Frequent/prolonged bleeding still occurs in a minority.

Drospirenone 4 mg 24/4

Scheduled bleeding, unscheduled bleeding, both or neither may occur. At about 6–9 months roughly 2–3 in 10 may be amenorrhoeic; total bleeding/spotting days may be similar to desogestrel.

21.2 Answer locking and re-submission control

The most predictable failure mode in any online prescribing pathway is a patient who is refused, learns which answer caused it, and submits again with that answer changed. A protocol that does not address this has screening that is, in effect, optional.

RULE LOCK-01 ON any clinical rejection: · The rejecting answer is LOCKED for the episode. No back-navigation. · The rejection is written to the patient record keyed to VERIFIED IDENTITY, not to email address, account or payment method. · A new consultation within 30 days pre-loads locked answers as read-only. Changing one requires prescriber contact, not a form edit. · A changed answer after a rejection is FLAGGED to the prescriber with both values and both timestamps. Never silently accepted. · Repeated attempts sharing an identity, address or payment instrument are surfaced for review. // A genuine change is possible — people do stop smoking and do obtain // blood pressure readings.
